About

Rodion Farulev

FARULEV is the artist name of Rodion Farulev, a cellist, composer and musician-developer whose work combines classical performance with electronic music, artificial intelligence and digital art.

Born in Seoul in 2004 into a family of musicians, he began the cello at four under his father’s guidance and appeared as a soloist with the National Symphony Orchestra of South Korea at the age of eight. He graduated from the Specialized Secondary Music School of the Saint Petersburg Conservatory, studied with Alexei Zhilin, and continues his education with Professor Kirill Rodin at the Tchaikovsky Conservatory.

He is a laureate of the Elena Obraztsova, Georgy Sviridov, Igor Stravinsky and Gnessin competitions and a recipient of a Grant of the President of the Russian Federation. On stage he plays a rare historic cello by Gasparo da Salò.

Full biography

At the core of his artistic practice is the cello as a space for dialogue between historical performance traditions and contemporary technologies, including spatial sound processing, generative systems, interactive formats and audiovisual dramaturgy.

After moving from Korea, he studied at the Rimsky-Korsakov Specialized Secondary Music School-Lyceum of the Saint Petersburg Conservatory with E. A. Dernova, while also studying piano at the S. S. Lyakhovitskaya Music School. He continued at the Saint Petersburg Conservatory in the class of Alexei Zhilin and is now studying with Professor Kirill Rodin at the Tchaikovsky Conservatory.

One of the defining events of his early career was Dmitri Kabalevsky’s First Cello Concerto with the Governor’s Symphony Orchestra of Saint Petersburg conducted by Anton Lubchenko at the Small Hall of the Saint Petersburg Philharmonia. He has performed at the Grand and Small Halls of the Philharmonia, the White Hall of the Sheremetev Palace, the House of Composers, the House of Radio, the Presidential Library and the Moscow and Saint Petersburg International Houses of Music, as well as in South Korea. His performances have been broadcast on radio and television.

He has taken part in the River of Talents, Music Team of Russia and Advanced Performance Courses programmes of the Saint Petersburg Music House, educational programmes at the Sirius Educational Center, the A Star Born in Saint Petersburg festival, the S. K. Gorkovenko Festival and Moscow Meets Friends, and in masterclasses with Sergei Roldugin, Alexander Knyazev, Alexander Ramm, Alexander Rudin, Miriam Prandi and Fyodor Amosov.

His digital-art work, which began under the name CELLEON, combines acoustic and electroacoustic cello, composition, spatial effects, AI-generated visuals and a virtual stage persona, placing classical material in dialogue with progressive, electronic and rock music. Projects include a rock suite for cello and jaw harp based on the Prelude from Bach’s First Cello Suite and works created with artificial intelligence, exploring human vulnerability, conflict, identity and environmental responsibility. He also leads the jazz trio BANG-BANG.

Alongside music he writes software and builds open prototypes: AI-based medical image analysis, machine-learning prediction of calibration drift in electronic circuit boards after thermal cycling, and digital productivity tools. In music technology he experiments with automated accompaniment, generative improvisation and interactive performance systems.

His long-term goal is an international interdisciplinary research center that brings together musicians, engineers, programmers and researchers to build new tools for music education and creative practice, and to study the cognitive effects of music.

Earlier projects appeared under the names CELLEON and Gentlkid; today all work is released as FARULEV.

Research & Technology

The Cello as a Hybrid Interface

Turning a four-hundred-year-old instrument into a living bridge between the performer and artificial intelligence.

Rodion works where performance, AI and engineering meet: the cello becomes an input for real-time machine listening, and the machine becomes a co-performer rather than a tool.
